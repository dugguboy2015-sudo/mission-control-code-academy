Field Manual

The Whole Ladder, Restated as Your Rubric

  • L1 (Sector 1): deployed to a real public URL; works on mobile.
  • L2 (Sector 4): secrets in env vars, never in code; real error and loading states; a README covering what it does, how to run it, what it uses.
  • L3 (Sector 5): real data and real auth; input validation; provably inside your provider's free tier.
  • L4 (Sector 7): AI calls go through your own proxy; guardrails on input and output; cost estimated AND tracked; a graceful fallback when the model fails or is slow.
  • L5 (Sectors 8-9): an eval suite that proves it works; observability/logging; explicit prompt-injection awareness in your design; a documented architecture diagram.
WHY EVERY LEVEL AT ONCE A real product doesn't get to skip L1 because it's focused on L5 — it needs to actually be reachable, on mobile, with a working README, AND have evals and observability. Production readiness stacks; it doesn't replace earlier levels, it adds to them.

Mission Challenge

Build a real AI-native product of your own choosing — small in scope is fine, but every item below must genuinely be true of the finished, deployed thing.

▸ HINT

Scope down before you cut corners on the ladder. A genuinely small, single-feature AI-native tool that hits every level cleanly is a far stronger finish than an ambitious app that skips half the checklist.

BONUS OBJECTIVE

Share your finished, deployed project's public URL with someone who has never seen it — a family member, a friend — and watch them use it without any explanation from you. Note every point of confusion; that's real, unfiltered user feedback, the kind no amount of testing it yourself ever produces.

⚠ COMMON MISTAKES

  • Polishing the AI feature (L4-L5) while skipping basics like a working README or mobile support (L1-L2).
  • Scoping a project too ambitiously to actually finish every ladder level cleanly.
  • Treating the architecture diagram as an afterthought instead of drawing it before finalizing the build.
  • Shipping without ever testing on a real device over a real, non-home network connection.
